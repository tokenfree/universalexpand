# UniversalExpand scientific review

UniversalExpand is broadly consistent with mainstream cosmology as an educational illustration, but it should not be described as entirely established science or a predictive simulation. Its central account of expansion, primordial light elements, recombination and later stellar enrichment is defensible. Several labels overstated certainty or blurred different physical processes; those have been corrected. The underlying background calculation is internally consistent with its stated approximation.

The important distinction is between an observed phenomenon, an inference within a model, a hypothesis and a visual convention. The application contains all four. An animation can illustrate a well-supported process without its particle motions, spatial distribution or timing being scientifically calculated.

## Scope and evidence standard

This review covers the twelve history chapters, material cards, 118-element atlas, four reaction demonstrations, expansion laboratory, numerical background, guide and scientific notebook. The literature cutoff is 10 September 2026. Journal research and expert reviews provide the scientific foundation; institutional educational pages remain useful supplementary reading. Recent preprints are identified separately and do not establish consensus by themselves.

The assessment combines inspection of the shipped source with literature comparison and an independent numerical calculation. It is a documented technical and literature review, not external peer review by a journal or certification by a scientific organization. It does not establish that every relevant paper has been considered, nor validate a precision cosmology pipeline that this application does not contain.

## Expansion and cosmological parameters

The distance rule d = aΔχ and its derivative v = Hd are appropriate for ideal comoving separations. Changing the coordinate origin does not identify a physical center of expansion. Local gravitationally bound systems and peculiar velocities require separate dynamics. Cosmological recession velocity is a coordinate-dependent quantity and should not be equated with a local special-relativistic velocity measurement. A horizon is not a material boundary. These distinctions support the app's existing explanation. [Davis & Lineweaver, 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0310808).

The baseline H₀ = 67.4 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ and Ωm = 0.315 are recognizable Planck ΛCDM values. The roughly 13.8-billion-year age is an inference conditional on such a model. Neither those numbers nor the commonly rounded 5% ordinary matter, 27% dark matter and 68% dark energy split should be presented as independent of cosmological assumptions or constant across cosmic history. [Planck Collaboration, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209).

Newer measurements do not yield a single model-independent replacement number. ACT DR6 reports H₀ = 68.43 ± 0.27 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ for its specified Planck–ACT, lensing and DESI DR2 combination under ΛCDM. That does not make 67.4 an arithmetic error in a declared Planck-based educational model. It does mean that the application must avoid calling it the uniquely agreed current measurement. [Louis et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14452).

Published DESI DR2 BAO results can be described by flat ΛCDM, but combinations with CMB and supernova samples favor an evolving-dark-energy extension to varying degrees. In the published analysis, the preference with supernovae ranges from 2.8 to 4.2 standard deviations depending on the sample. This is a meaningful challenge, not a settled finding that dark energy evolves. [DESI Collaboration, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14738).

The subsequent DR2 IV Lyα analysis was available as a 2026 preprint, with no journal reference established at this review date. It supplies further conditional constraints rather than a universal replacement cosmology. The app now labels that status instead of allowing a news link to carry the implication of journal consensus. [Adame et al., 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27410).

## Early-universe chronology

| Chapter | Assessment | Necessary interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum frontier | Unknown regime, correctly bounded after revision | No established account of the ultimate origin or a state “before” the hot Big Bang |
| Possible inflation | Hypothesis, explicitly labeled | No measured absolute timestamp, identified inflaton or unique reheating history |
| Hot particle plasma | Standard thermal-history illustration | Temperature depends on age and relativistic particle content |
| Protons and neutrons | Broadly supported | The QCD crossover is extended; the chapter time is approximate |
| Neutrino decoupling | Supported thermal-history inference | Not an instantaneous event; background neutrinos have indirect observational support |
| Light nuclei assemble | Supported with known tensions | Primordial nuclear reactions occur before neutral-atom formation |
| Hydrogen recombination | Supported, terminology corrected | A finite visibility epoch, not the creation of all photons or all neutral species at once |
| Dark ages | Representative phase | Gas collapse and molecular chemistry are heterogeneous |
| First stars | Approximate theoretical chronology | 200 million years is a teaching landmark, not a universal first-star date |
| Galaxies and enrichment | Supported broad history | Reionization and enrichment are spatially uneven and overlap |
| Sun and planets | Rounded chronology is appropriate | Solar System formation is a local, extended history |
| Today | Model-conditional age | The application rounds the age and fixes a = 1 |

Inflation is motivated by cosmological observations and constrained by them, but a unique mechanism and clock time are not established. The application appropriately retains it as a possible chapter rather than removing uncertainty through a precise-looking number. The internal anchors remain solely to order and interpolate playback. [Ellis, Vennin & Wands, PDG 2026 edition](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-inflation.pdf).

For a radiation-dominated thermal history, the PDG relation t(s)T(MeV)² ≈ 2.4/√g* gives approximately 5.59 × 10¹⁵ K at 10⁻¹² seconds with g* = 106.75. The displayed rounded value is consequently ~6 × 10¹⁵ K. This is a conditional estimate, not a measured temperature at that epoch. The fixed radiation term used by the app at late times cannot model every change in relativistic species. [Olive & Peacock, PDG 2026 edition](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-bbang-cosmology.pdf).

Lattice QCD gives a chiral crossover temperature of about 156.5 ± 1.5 MeV at zero baryon chemical potential. This supports an approximate trillion-kelvin hadronization chapter, while not establishing a sharp moment when all hadrons suddenly appear. The notebook's prior microsecond range was narrowed to an order-of-magnitude 10⁻⁵-second landmark and linked to the QCD paper. [Bazavov et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08235).

The relic neutrino background has strong indirect support but has not been directly detected as a primordial background. Massive neutrinos also do not remain exactly equivalent to a fixed massless radiation term at all epochs. The app's disclosure of that approximation is essential. [Lesgourgues & Verde, PDG 2026 edition](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-neutrinos-in-cosmology.pdf).

Primordial hydrogen and helium mass fractions of roughly 75% and 25% are suitable introductory values for ordinary matter. Deuterium, helium-3 and lithium-7 are traces; much lithium-7 originates through beryllium-7 decay. Lithium predictions and old-star measurements remain discrepant. Stellar depletion is an important proposed resolution, but the interface should not state that the issue is definitively solved. [Fields, Molaro & Sarkar, PDG 2026 edition](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-bbang-nucleosynthesis.pdf).

Recombination calculations require multiple atomic levels and radiation transport. The simple hydrogen reaction is useful as a schematic but does not calculate the ionization history, last-scattering visibility function or CMB spectrum. Photons existed before this epoch; reduced scattering lets the existing background propagate. [Chluba & Thomas, 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3631).

The present temperature 2.7255 K is an appropriate rounding of the measured 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K value. The app's later temperature display refers to this background radiation, not to the gas, stars or planets shown in the same scene. [Fixsen, 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.1955).

## Stars, enrichment and the element atlas

First-star formation is a local outcome of gravitational collapse, cooling and feedback. The mass distribution and formation histories of Population III stars remain research questions; the 200-million-year marker is not an observationally established first birthday. A detection of an early galaxy likewise does not establish when all galaxies began. [Klessen & Glover, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-astro-071221-053453).

The one-billion-year chapter says that ultraviolet light reionizes **much** intergalactic gas. This is appropriately cautious. Quasar-sightline work finds reionization-related fluctuations persisting to approximately z = 5.3, or 1.1 billion years in its cosmology. It would be misleading to relabel one billion years as an exact universal completion time. [Bosman et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03699).

The atlas's broad families are reasonable introductions. Stellar burning, explosions, neutron capture and radioactive decay contribute in overlapping ways, and a chemical element can contain isotopes with different histories. The absence of universal percentages and exact first-production dates is a strength. The atlas is not a substitute for a nuclide chart or an isotope-resolved yield calculation. [Arcones & Thielemann, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00159-022-00146-x).

Neutron-star mergers are an observed site associated with rapid neutron-capture material, but the contributions of different event classes and their history remain uncertain. The application does not claim that all gold or all heavy elements came from a single class of event. That qualification should remain. [Cowan et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.93.015002).

The cited identification of strontium in AT2017gfo is a published spectroscopic result, not direct weighing of every element in the ejecta. Line identification involves radiative-transfer and atomic-physics modeling. [Watson et al., 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1676-3). A later published analysis tests a competing helium explanation and finds it inconsistent with the relevant spectral evolution; newer work continues to refine the interpretation. This does not warrant discarding the historical strontium evidence. [Sneppen et al., 2024](https://repository.gsi.de/record/358851/files/aa51450-24.pdf?subformat=pdfa).

The approximately 4.6-billion-year Solar System age is valid rounding. High-precision dating of early calcium–aluminium-rich inclusions gives 4567.30 ± 0.16 million years in the cited study. Those early solids are a chronology anchor, not proof that the Sun, Earth and every planet formed simultaneously. [Connelly et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23118187/).

The 118 atomic numbers, symbols and names follow IUPAC's recognized table. This authority addresses nomenclature, not astrophysical production fractions. The display's detached lanthanoid and actinoid rows are a conventional layout choice; their position does not encode chronology. [IUPAC periodic table](https://iupac.org/what-we-do/periodic-table-of-elements/).

## Numerical and visual validation

The app evaluates H(a) = H₀√(Ωr a⁻⁴ + Ωm a⁻³ + ΩΛ), with Ωr = 0.000092 and ΩΛ = 0.684908. It integrates dt/dln(a) on 8,000 logarithmic intervals and interpolates to obtain a at a requested time. Curvature is zero. These equations describe the selected homogeneous approximation; the implementation contains no likelihood fit to observations.

An independent check in `verify-science.cjs` changes both integration variable and quadrature. It integrates a/[H₀√(Ωr + Ωm a + ΩΛa⁴)] with composite Simpson integration on a uniform grid. Convergence is checked with 50,000 and 100,000 intervals, and six scale factors between 0.0005 and 1 are compared with the app's inverse function.

| Quantity | Independent calculation | Application |
|---|---:|---:|
| Age at a = 1, billion years | 13.790692524 | 13.790699341 |
| CMB temperature at 380,000 years, K | 2932.377409 | 2932.381968 |
| Redshift at 380,000 years | 1074.904388 | 1074.906061 |

These differences are small numerical-integration effects: approximately 0.00005% in present age and 0.00016% in the displayed recombination temperature. They do not measure how close the simplified physical model is to the real universe. The roughly 2,932 K result is compatible with the rounded 3,000 K chapter label, but it is not a recombination calculation because the model contains no atomic kinetics.

The final chapter's 13.8-billion-year label is rounded and slightly exceeds the integrated age. Clamping a to 1 is a documented presentation convention. Extra displayed decimal places describe calculation output; they must not be interpreted as observational confidence intervals.

The 13,720 scene sprites are procedurally generated tracers. Their clustering is interpolated, their colors are explanatory and their expansion is visually compressed. They are neither a measured catalog nor a gravity simulation. The displayed volume is a simultaneous cosmic-time slice, not the light cone an observer would actually see. This disclosure is necessary even when the qualitative history is correct.

The expansion laboratory consistently changes distances by a common factor. Its fixed H₀ comparison isolates the distance law; moving the scale slider is not evolving H(t). Similarly, the reaction animations do not calculate cross-sections, branching ratios, released photon energies or rates. The quark diagram is schematic confinement, deuterium formation is a radiative capture, hydrogen is atomic recombination and carbon illustrates the triple-alpha sequence. The cloud and nucleon geometry must remain explicitly symbolic.

Automated checks pass for the independent background calculation, all twelve chapter transitions, all 118 atlas entries, four reaction paths, research anchors and server behavior. Those checks detect implementation regressions; they cannot establish scientific consensus or validate quantum trajectories that are not modeled.

## Remaining scientific limits and useful additions

The most useful next scientific expansion would be an isotope view with isotope-specific sources and explicit uncertainty. It would need a curated dataset, rather than extending one broad element category into invented percentages. A second useful addition would show the difference between an illustrative age landmark and an inferred distribution or extended transition. These additions are recommendations, not capabilities already shipped.

For a precision cosmology mode, the next requirement would be a validated background and recombination solver with massive-neutrino evolution, consistent thermal histories and clearly selected parameter sets. Merely adding digits to the existing display would not provide that accuracy. For a predictive structure-formation mode, gravitational dynamics and gas physics would be a separate substantial project.

The current release can defensibly say: “An educational reconstruction based on established cosmology, with model-dependent parameters, approximate milestones and explicitly schematic animations.” It should not say “everything is scientifically proven,” “all elements simulated accurately,” or “the latest research has settled the nature of dark energy.” A named external cosmologist and nuclear/atomic-physics reviewer would be appropriate before any formal scientific endorsement.
